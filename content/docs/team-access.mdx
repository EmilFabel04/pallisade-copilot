# Team & Access Control

Pallisade uses role-based access control (RBAC) to manage what each team member can do. Roles are assigned at two levels: the organization level and the pipeline level.

## Organization Roles

Every team member has exactly one organization role that determines their base permissions across all pipelines.

| Role | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Owner** | Full control over the organization, billing, and all settings. Can transfer ownership. Only one Owner per organization. |
| **Admin** | Manage team members, integrations, and settings. Cannot delete the organization or manage billing. |
| **Manager** | Run scans, configure monitors, and approve fixes. Cannot manage team members or organization settings. |
| **Engineer** | Run scans, use the copilot, and apply fixes. Day-to-day operations access. |
| **Viewer** | Read-only access to reports, findings, dashboards, and the copilot chat history. |

<Callout type="info">
The role hierarchy determines which roles a user can manage. Owners can manage everyone. Admins can manage Managers, Engineers, and Viewers. Managers cannot manage other team members.
</Callout>

## Pipeline Roles

In addition to organization roles, you can assign per-pipeline roles to control access to specific pipelines.

| Pipeline Role | Permissions |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Owner** | Full control over the pipeline, including settings, integrations, and access management |
| **Editor** | Run monitors, use Scout, apply fixes, and edit pipeline configuration |
| **Viewer** | Read-only access to the pipeline's data, monitors, and lineage |

Pipeline roles work alongside organization roles. A team member's effective permissions are the union of their organization role and their pipeline role.

<Callout type="tip">
Use pipeline roles to restrict sensitive pipelines. For example, give an Engineer organization-wide access but restrict them to Viewer on the production-finance pipeline.
</Callout>

## Inviting Team Members

### Sending an Invitation

1. Go to the **Team** page from the dashboard sidebar.
2. Click **Invite Member**.
3. Enter the person's email address.
4. Select an organization role (Admin, Manager, Engineer, or Viewer).
5. Click **Send Invitation**.

The invitee receives an email with a link to join your organization. The invitation expires after 7 days.

### Invitation States

| State | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Pending** | Invitation sent, not yet accepted |
| **Accepted** | User has joined the organization |
| **Expired** | Invitation was not accepted within 7 days |

You can resend or revoke pending invitations from the Team page.

<Callout type="warning">
Only Owners and Admins can invite new members. The invitee's role cannot exceed the inviter's role -- an Admin cannot invite someone as an Owner.
</Callout>

## Managing Team Members

### Changing a Member's Role

1. Go to the **Team** page.
2. Find the member in the list.
3. Click their current role to open the role selector.
4. Select the new role.
5. Confirm the change.

**Restrictions:**
- You can only assign roles below your own level.
- You cannot change the Owner's role (ownership must be transferred instead).
- Downgrading your own role is irreversible unless an Owner upgrades you.

### Removing a Member

1. Go to the **Team** page.
2. Find the member in the list.
3. Click **Remove**.
4. Confirm the removal.

Removed members immediately lose access to all pipelines and data. Their past activity remains in the audit log.

### Transferring Ownership

Only the current Owner can transfer ownership:

1. Go to the **Team** page.
2. Click **Transfer Ownership**.
3. Select the team member to receive ownership.
4. Confirm the transfer.

After transfer, the previous Owner is downgraded to Admin. There can only be one Owner at a time.

## Audit Log

The audit log records significant actions across your organization for compliance and security review.

### What Gets Logged

| Event Type | Examples |
|------------|---------|
| **Team changes** | Member invited, role changed, member removed, ownership transferred |
| **Integration changes** | Integration created, edited, deleted, connection tested |
| **Pipeline changes** | Pipeline created, deleted, access changed |
| **Monitor changes** | Monitor created, edited, deleted, schedule changed |
| **Fix applications** | Change plan created, validated, applied, PR opened |
| **Settings changes** | Alert channels configured, organization settings updated |

### Viewing the Audit Log

1. Go to **Settings** from the dashboard sidebar.
2. Select the **Audit Log** tab.
3. Browse events by date, type, or actor.

Each log entry shows:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Timestamp** | When the action occurred |
| **Actor** | Who performed the action (name and email) |
| **Action** | What was done (e.g., "Invited member", "Created monitor") |
| **Target** | What was affected (e.g., member email, pipeline name, monitor name) |
| **Details** | Additional context (e.g., role assigned, old vs. new values) |

<Callout type="info">
The audit log is available to Owners, Admins, and Managers. Engineers and Viewers do not have access.
</Callout>

## Best Practices

1. **Use the least-privilege principle.** Assign Viewer to team members who only need to read dashboards and reports. Promote to Engineer when they need to run queries and use Scout.
2. **Restrict production pipelines.** Use pipeline-level Viewer roles for sensitive production data pipelines, even for team members who have Engineer access organization-wide.
3. **Review the audit log regularly.** Check for unexpected role changes, integration modifications, or fix applications.
4. **Use separate service accounts.** Connect warehouses with dedicated service accounts rather than personal credentials. This way, removing a team member does not break warehouse connections.
